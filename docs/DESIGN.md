# Design notes

**Target:** Minecraft 1.21.1 · NeoForge 21.1.265 · ModDevGradle 2.0.148 · Java 21 · Parchment 2024.11.17
**Mod id:** `reikos_signal_tower` · **Package:** `com.reiko.signaltower`

## Rules

- **Frequencies** are whole numbers from 1 to 999,999,999, stored in each transmitter, receiver and repeater's block entity (NBT key `frequency`). New blocks start on 1.
- **Reach** belongs to transmitters and repeaters: `baseReach + reachPerBlockOfHeight × max(0, y − seaLevel)`, capped by `maxReach` if that's above 0. Defaults are 32 and 8, with no cap.
- **Links:** a receiver hears a transmitter or repeater on its frequency if their *horizontal* distance is at most its reach. Height difference doesn't matter.
- **Radio Repeater:**
  - Listens for its frequency at its own position (like a receiver there) and sends the strongest signal it hears out again with its own reach, at the same strength. No redstone input.
  - It hears transmitters, remote pulses and latches, and other repeaters, but only signals that trace back to a real transmitter or remote press: two repeaters can't keep each other on.
  - Like a transmitter, it relays remote presses on any frequency when it's within the player's reach (`towersRelayRemotes`).
  - `LIT` block state (light level 7, amber sparks) while repeating. Recipe: Radio Receiver + Radio Transmitter (shapeless).
- **Wall Redstone on ceilings and round corners (v0.52.0):** `facing` takes every value but up; down hangs it under a block (needs a sturdy bottom face), its arms fixed to the compass (up = south, down = north, left = west, right = east, as its model is turned x 270). An arm also connects round an outside corner: when the space beside it in that direction isn't a redstone conductor, to Wall Redstone in the space past that edge stuck to the same block (the next wall, or the ceiling under it), or over a wall's top edge to vanilla dust on the block. Corner neighbours count as wires (their power − 1). Vanilla dust doesn't see us diagonally; dust on a conductor block reaches the wall through the block anyway.
- **Dish Rotator (v0.52.0):** a `RadioMastBlock` with FACING (the placer's look). In a tower's column, the one nearest the top sets a Dish Antenna's aim to `segment(facing) + strength` (mod 16; north 0, east 4, south 8, west 12), the strength being `getBestNeighborSignal` at the rotator. Its neighbours' redstone isn't read as the tower's input. Fitting a dish takes the rotator's aim; re-aiming one by hand is refused while a rotator holds it. The mast builder can put one in (arrow = the player's look). Recipe: Radio Mast + Comparator + Copper Ingot.
- **Mast builder (v0.51.0):** right-clicking a Radio Mast, or a full-size tower top, with a Radio Mast (not sneaking) walks up the mast column to the first block that isn't a mast; if that's a tower top it and whatever rides on it (an Aviation Beacon standing on it, or a Lightning Rod) move up one block with their block entities, and a mast goes where the top was; with no tower top, a rider still moves up, or the mast goes on top of the column. Needs a replaceable block above (inside the build limit), may-build rights, and on someone else's private frequency membership (like antennas). One mast used up (not in creative).
- **Radio Telegraph (v0.51.0):** a `RadioBlock` desk (FACING, POWERED). A message (StringUtil-filtered, trimmed, ≤ 64 characters; one per 40 ticks per telegraph) goes out over the telegraph's own reach (`reachAt` its position), every tower in that reach (`remoteCoverage`, `towersRelayRemotes`) and repeaters on its frequency (`withRepeaters`; not converters). It doesn't change the frequency's strength. Each dimension keeps the last 16 per frequency (saved). Readers inside it: pagers on the frequency (chat + action bar, two beeps), other loaded telegraphs on it (3 clicks, POWERED for 20 ticks: weak power out, strong into the block below), Status Boards (the last message under the row for 6,000 ticks); telegraphs' screens show the last 6 that covered them. Private: only members send (refusal + intruder alert `TELEGRAPH`); encrypted: only members' (or member-tuned) readers get the text. Recipe: Radio Transmitter + Radio Receiver + Book and Quill.
- **Ender Antenna (v0.50.0):** an antenna tier (`ender`, multiplier 1) only full-size transmitters (plain, timer, recorder) take. Such a transmitter also sends into one other dimension (default: the Nether from the Overworld, else the Overworld; another Ender Antenna steps to the next in the server's order): heard there as a transmitter at `x × scale, y (clamped to the build limits), z × scale` (scale = `DimensionType.getTeleportationScale`), with its power (0 while down), its clear-weather reach in its own weather, fade, no hills, no height lift. Repeaters there relay it. With power cost on it uses twice the charge.
- **Solar Panel (v0.50.0):** charges the Radio Battery directly under it once a second by `solarChargePerSecond` (3) × daylight / 15, daylight being a Daylight Detector's reading at the panel (0 without sky light); whole units, the rest carried over.
- **Power cost (v0.49.0):** with `towersNeedPower` (on by default), a full-size transmitter (plain, timer, recorder), repeater, converter or jammer needs a Radio Battery with charge next to its top or next to any mast in the unbroken column under it; otherwise it's out of power (sends, relays, jams nothing; light off; doesn't relay remote presses). While it sends (a transmitter or jammer with redstone, a repeater hearing something, a gate sending) it uses `chargePerSecondPer64Blocks` (1) × stored reach / 64 a second (antenna multiplier included, a dish counts as 1), paid in whole units from its batteries in turn each second; when they run dry it goes quiet. Batteries hold `batteryCapacity` (100,000); Redstone Dust gives `chargePerRedstone` (1,000), a Block of Redstone 9×; FE gives 1 charge per `fePerCharge` (10). Compact transmitters, motion sensors, keypads, remotes and listeners are free. Only loaded towers are checked; the out-of-power set is saved.
- **Coverage Map (v0.49.0):** a `MapItem` with a frequency and a zoom (0–4, default 2). Right-click: a map square lined up like a vanilla map's (`MapItemSavedData.createFresh`) where the player stands, reused if it's the same square and zoom; while held, 4 rows a tick are painted with `listen(level, f, pixel centre on the ground (height map) or the holder's y if unloaded, holder)`: 13–15 emerald, 10–12 lime, 7–9 yellow, 4–6 orange, 1–3 red (brightness high/normal/low within a band), 0 blank. Each pass starts by marking towers on the frequency (red marker transmitter, blue repeater/converter, red X jammer; up to 32).
- **Signal fade (v0.48.0):** a signal from a transmitter (not compact ones or motion sensors) or a repeater / converter heard by a final listener (receiver of any kind, the pager / meter / scanner holder, a jammer's jamming) at fraction f = distance / reach (the link reach: weather, height, dish) is `sent` up to `signalFadeStartPercent` (50) of the reach, then `round(sent × (1 − t × signalFadeAtEdge / 15))` with t = (f − start) / (1 − start), at least 1 (14 by default: 15 → 1 at the edge). Repeaters and converters (listening, gate input) hear it unfaded, so they restore full strength. Order: fade, then hills, then jamming. Remote presses and keypads don't fade.
- **Dish Antenna (v0.48.0):** an antenna tier (`dish`) with an aim (`rotation`, 0–15: north, clockwise; set from the player's look when fitted, and from the placer's look when placing a tower item carrying one). Its multiplier is 1 (stored reach = bare reach); toward a listener whose horizontal offset is within `dishConeDegrees` (15) of the aim, the tower's clear-weather reach is `max(reach, reach × dishReachMultiplier)` (3, capped by maxReach). Applies to transmitters (plain, timer, recorder), repeaters, converters and jammers, and to remote presses a dish tower relays. Fitting a dish on a dish turns it (nothing used up).
- **Receiver height (v0.47.0):** a listener (receiver of any kind, repeater / converter listening, the pager / meter / scanner holder's block) higher (greater y) than a tower hears it within `max(tower's reach, ear)`, where `ear` = `reachAt(listener)` (base + perBlock × height above sea level, capped) × `receiverHeightPercent` (100); then the tower's weather applies. Not for compact transmitters or motion sensors; not for jammers or remote presses. Same height or lower: the tower's reach as before.
- **Lightning (v0.47.0):** each second in a thunderstorm, each loaded tower (full-size transmitter, repeater, converter, jammer) with `isRainingAt(top + 1)` (open sky, rain not snow) is struck with chance `lightningStrikesPerHour` / 3600 × height / 64 (6 by default; none at or below sea level). A Lightning Rod within 2 blocks (any direction) of the top takes the hit (its pulse; the tower stays on); otherwise the tower is knocked out for `lightningOutageSeconds` (5): sends, relays, jams and relays remote presses nothing, light off, sparks; back by itself. The mod's bolts are visual only (no fire, no harm); any other bolt joining the world on a tower's top (or the block above it) counts, with the same rod rule.
- **Weather (v0.46.0):** while it rains or snows where a tower stands (by biome: `Biome.getPrecipitationAt != NONE`; never in deserts, savannas, badlands, the Nether or the End), the reach of transmitters (not compact ones or motion sensors), repeaters, converters and jammers is `rainReachLoss` percent shorter (15), in a thunderstorm `thunderReachLoss` (30), rounded. Keypads and remotes aren't affected. Applied when the network measures reach, so stored reach stays the dry one.
- **Terrain (v0.46.0):** the straight line from a tower's top (y + 1) to the middle of the listener's block (a receiver, a repeater or converter listening, or the pager / meter / scanner holder's feet block); every `terrainBlocksPerStrength` (8) blocks of it, measured horizontally, that run through ground (a block that stops movement, or a liquid; not leaves, not air) take 1 off that signal's strength (0–15). The first and last 8 blocks don't count; up to 64 points are checked; unloaded chunks count as clear. A repeater passes on what it heard after hills. Remote presses ignore hills. The strongest signal after hills wins (ties: the one that started stronger, so a fully blocked one still shows its loss).
- **Radio Jammer (v0.46.0):** a full-size tower top (masts, antennas, tuner like a transmitter) that sends nothing. Its redstone input (0–15, from itself or its masts) is its jamming strength `j`; inside its reach (`jammerReachPercent` = 50% of a transmitter's at that height with that antenna; weather applies) everything listening on its frequency hears `max(0, heard − (j − hills between))` (the strongest jammer counts). Repeaters and converters inside it pass on less. On someone else's private frequency a non-member can't tune it (refusal "it would jam …" + TUNE alert); one tuned or placed by a non-member stays silent (SILENT alert on placement). Recipe: Radio Transmitter + Note Block + Redstone Dust (shapeless).
- **Antennas (v0.45.0):** a block state property `antenna` (none / iron / gold / diamond / netherite) on full-size transmitters (plain, timer, recorder), repeaters and converters. The tower's reach = (baseReach + reachPerBlockOfHeight × height) × the antenna's multiplier (config `ironAntennaReach` 1.5, `goldAntennaReach` 2, `diamondAntennaReach` 3, `netheriteAntennaReach` 4), rounded, then capped by `maxReach`. Fitted by right-clicking the tower with the antenna item (uses one up outside creative; the replaced one comes back); sneak + empty-hand right-click takes it off. On someone else's private frequency (the one the tower sends on) only members may (refusal + TUNE alert). The item keeps it (only when it has one). Recipes: Iron (shaped: iron ingot over three iron ingots over redstone), Gold = Iron + 2 gold + redstone, Diamond = Gold + 2 diamonds, Netherite = Diamond + netherite ingot (shapeless).
- **Radio Lamp (v0.45.0):** a receiver whose `POWER` (what it hears) is its light level; outputs no redstone. `COLOR` (16 dye colors, block state; a dye right-click changes it, using one outside creative). The item keeps frequency and color. Recipe: Radio Receiver + Redstone Lamp (shapeless).
- **Radio Signal Recorder (v0.44.0):** a full-size transmitter (reach by height, masts, relays remote presses) that broadcasts a recording instead of its redstone input. Record waits for its "Records from" frequency (≠ the one it plays on) to turn on where it stands, as its tuner hears it, then keeps every change of the strength heard there, to the tick, until Stop, 600 ticks (30 s) or 256 changes; the length runs to Stop, so trailing silence counts. The old recording is replaced only when the new one starts. Once mode (default): a redstone rising edge into it or its mast column plays it from the start (again from the start if already playing); Loop mode: plays over and over while powered, stops (output 0) when the power goes. Play plays once; Clear forgets. Placing or loading while powered isn't a pulse. Any button makes the player its tuner; on someone else's private frequency only members may change it (refusal + TUNE alert); someone else's encrypted Records from is recorded as silence. The item keeps both frequencies, the mode and the recording. Recipe: Radio Transmitter + Radio Receiver + Book (shapeless).
- **Aviation Beacon (v0.44.0):** a light on a floor, wall or ceiling face; it survives on any sturdy face, a Radio Mast or a tower top (full-size transmitter of any kind, repeater, converter). Settings in its block state: When (night = day time 12,542–23,459 or rain, always in fixed-time dimensions / always / while its tower sends), Pattern (blink 20 of 40 ticks, flash 3 of 30, steady), Color (red, white). Its tower: the tower top it's attached to, or the top of the mast column it's attached to; "sending" = the top's `POWERED` / `LIT`. Lit = When holds and the pattern is on at the world's game time, so every beacon blinks in step. Drawn by the client with a glow that grows with distance and isn't fogged. No redstone, no frequency; anyone may change it. Pops off when its support goes; the item keeps the settings (`copy_state`). Recipe (shaped, makes 2): red stained glass over redstone, lamp, redstone, over an iron ingot.
- **Radio Speaker (v0.43.0):** a receiver (outputs what it hears) that plays a vanilla sound on each turn-on of its frequency: Bell, Chime, Ding, Doorbell (two notes a major third apart, 6 ticks between), Pling, Bass, Beep; note 0–24 (note-block pitch, default 12 = F♯4); volume quiet 0.4 / normal 1 (16 blocks) / loud 3 (48 blocks); once, or every 20 ticks while it hears its frequency. A note particle each time. Placing, loading or re-tuning while on isn't a turn-on. Screen (empty hand) with a client-only Test. The item keeps the settings. Recipe: Radio Receiver + Note Block (shapeless), and back.
- **Radio Status Board (v0.43.0):** a 14 × 14 × 2 panel on the side of a block (like a wall sign; pops off when the block behind goes). Up to 4 rows (0 = empty): light (green on, dark red off, grey deaf), name (or number; the number small under a name), strength or "off" / "enc", full bright, drawn within 32 blocks. Hears as whoever placed it or last pressed Done in its screen (encrypted frequencies). A display only: no redstone, not tracked as a receiver. The item keeps the rows. Recipe: Radio Receiver + Redstone Lamp + Glass Pane (shapeless).
- **Radio Timer Transmitter (v0.42.0):** a full-size transmitter (reach by height, masts, relays remote presses) that sends 15 on a schedule instead of its redstone input. Repeating: every 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 15, 30 s or 1, 2, 5, 10, 20 min (default 10 s), a pulse of one of the remote's pulse lengths (default 1 s), at most half the interval; the rhythm starts when it's placed or set and follows game time through reloads. Daily: on from one whole in-game hour to another (default 18:00–06:00, by the time of day; equal = never). Any redstone signal into it or its mast column pauses it. Set in its screen (empty hand); on someone else's private frequency only members may change the schedule (refusal + TUNE alert). The item keeps the schedule. Recipe: Radio Transmitter + Clock (shapeless), and back.
- **Radio Counter Receiver (v0.42.0):** counts turn-ons (silent → any strength) of its frequency and outputs 15 when the count reaches its target (1–16, 20, 24, 32, 48, 64; default 3): a 1 s pulse and back to 0, or on until reset. Forget after (the delay receiver's times; default none): the count starts over after that long without a turn-on. Reset frequency (optional): its turn-on clears the count and switches it off; so does Reset count in its screen. Tracked as a receiver on both frequencies; the screen's Done makes the player its tuner (like the logic receiver). Placing, loading, re-tuning or a deafness change isn't a turn-on. The count is saved; the item keeps the settings, not the count. Recipe: Radio Receiver + Hopper (shapeless), and back.
- **Signal gate (v0.41.0):** a converter setting, Pass / At least / Below with a level 1–15. Pass sends what it hears; At least sends what it hears only at the level or more (a comparator); Below sends 15 while it hears less than the level (at 1, a NOT gate). A gated converter is a source, not a relay: it sends what its block entity last decided, changing at most every 2 ticks, so loops (a NOT gate on its own frequency) blink instead of hanging, and it doesn't carry remote presses. The item keeps the gate. Set in the converter's screen.
- **Radio Delay Receiver (v0.41.0):** outputs 15 or 0. Delay (none, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 30 or 60 s; default 1 s): every on/off change of its frequency comes through that much later, in order. Hold (same times; default none): stays on that long after its delayed input turns off. Set in its screen (empty-hand click); the tuner sets the frequency. Timings aren't saved mid-change: on load or re-tune it starts from what it hears. Deaf on someone else's encrypted frequency. The item keeps the delay and hold. Recipe: Radio Receiver + Redstone Repeater (shapeless), and back on its own.
- **Radio Logic Receiver (v0.40.0):** a receiver on two frequencies: AND (both on; the weaker strength), OR (either; the stronger) or XOR (exactly one; its strength). Set in its screen (empty-hand click); the tuner sets and copies the first. Tracked as a receiver on both. Deaf on someone else's encrypted frequency counts as that input off. The item keeps both frequencies and the mode. Recipe: 2 Radio Receivers + Nether Quartz (shapeless).
- **Signal Meter (v0.40.0):** a handheld reading one frequency where the holder stands, five times a second: the strength a receiver there would get, towers in reach, and blocks to the edge of the farthest-reaching sender on it (like the reach ring: `reach - floor(distance)` inside, else out of reach; transmitters count when off, silenced or deaf relays don't). Panel right of the hotbar, Geiger tick (every 20 ticks at strength 1 down to every 2 at 15; right-click toggles it), needle model. Sneak + right-click air: screen (favorites, Tick, Directory); sneak + right-click a radio block: the frequency it sends on; sneak + scroll; scanner Tune. Encrypted for the holder: "Encrypted by Dev", nothing measured. Recipe: Compact Radio Receiver + Redstone Comparator + Iron Ingot (shapeless).
- **Radio Inverted Receiver (v0.39.0):** a receiver that outputs 15 while it hears nothing on its frequency and 0 while it hears any strength. Deaf (someone else's encrypted frequency) means it hears nothing, so it stays at 15 and gives nothing away. Lit (light 5) while it outputs. Recipe: Radio Receiver + Redstone Torch (shapeless), and back on its own.
- **Radio Motion Sensor (v0.39.0):**
  - Button-sized, attaches like the compact transmitter (pops off with its support, keeping its frequency; pistons break it). Sends 15 on its frequency while it sees a player, over the compact reach (`compactTransmitterReach`), not a relay tower; the redstone around it is ignored.
  - Sees: non-spectator players whose body middle is within its range of a point 0.3 in front of its face, with a clear `COLLIDER` line from there to their eyes or body middle. Looks every 10 ticks; keeps sending 40 ticks after it last saw someone. The nearest one seen when it turns on is named in the log (`CauseKind.SENSOR`, with the sensor's position, like a keypad).
  - Owner = placer (and tuner). Only the owner or an operator changes its range (2 / 4 / 6 / 8 / 12 / 16, default 6) and mode (Everyone / Strangers only) or re-tunes it. Strangers only ignores the owner and, on a private frequency, its owner and members (`Claim.allows`: friends, group members, guests).
  - Others get a readout (frequency, owner, reach) and Jade without range, mode or names. Recipe: Compact Radio Transmitter + Observer (shapeless).
- **Compact Radio Transmitter (v0.38.0):** button-sized, attaches like the compact receiver (pops off with its support, keeping its frequency). Broadcasts the strongest neighbour signal (`getBestNeighborSignal`, no mast column) over a fixed `compactTransmitterReach` (default 16, capped by `maxReach`), whatever its height. Not a relay tower for remote presses. Recipe: Radio Transmitter ↔ Compact Radio Transmitter (shapeless, both ways).
- **Keypad guest codes (v0.38.0):**
  - The owner's keypad screen has **Guest codes...**: up to 8 extra codes, each with a name (up to 16 characters), 4 to 8 digits, a use count (1, 3, 10 or any) and a real-time limit (1 hour, 1 day, 1 week or any). Not the keypad's own code nor another guest code; names differ (ignoring case); the main code can't be set to a guest code.
  - A guest code works like the main code until its uses or time run out, then answers "That guest code is used up" / "has expired" without counting towards the lockout. Spent ones stay listed until removed.
  - The owner, if online and not the typer, gets a chat line per use ("Code Keypad at 406, -59, -9: Alex used the guest code "Plumber" (2 uses left)"); the log's What says "Guest code: Plumber".
- **Code Keypad (v0.37.0):**
  - A thin 8 × 10 panel that attaches to a wall, floor or ceiling like a button (pops off with its support; pistons break it). Recipe: Remote Control + Iron Ingot + Stone Button (shapeless).
  - Whoever places it owns it (and is its tuner). Only the owner, or an operator, sets the code (4 to 8 digits) and the pulse length (the remote's presets, 0.1 to 10 s, default 1 s), and re-tunes it ("This Code Keypad is Dev's: only they can tune it"). The tuner is the only item that keeps its own use on it; anything else in hand opens its screen (sneaking with an item uses the item, so remotes still copy its frequency).
  - The right code sends a pulse on its frequency from the keypad's position, like a remote pressed there (its own height-based reach, plus towers in that reach relaying it), and for the pulse it's `POWERED` (green display, light 4), giving 15 weak power around and strong power into the block it's on, like a button.
  - Three wrong codes in a row lock that player out of that keypad for 30 seconds (in memory, per player), and the owner gets an intruder alert (kind KEYPAD) with the keypad's position, on any frequency; not for the owner's own mistakes.
  - On someone else's private frequency it sends only if its tuner is a member; otherwise the right code does nothing ("Silent: …"), and placing it there tells the placer and alerts the frequency's owner (SILENT). Anyone with the code can use a member's keypad.
  - The code never leaves the server: block entities aren't synced, the screen packet only says whether there is one, the save message only gives its length. The item keeps only the frequency (owner and code are reset by the next placement).
- **Reach preview ring (v0.37.0):**
  - While a player holds a Frequency Tuner (either hand), looking at a transmitter, repeater, converter or Code Keypad, or a mast under one, within 128 blocks shows a dotted ring on the ground at its reach (radius reach + ½ from the middle of its block), in its light's color, and two lines above the hotbar: what it is with its frequency and reach, and "You're inside: N blocks to the edge" / "You're right at the edge" / "You're N blocks outside".
  - The ring stays on that sender while the tuner is held (look at another to move it; put the tuner away to clear it); only that player sees it, within their render distance.
- **Guest passes (v0.36.0):**
  - The owner's audit has **Guests...**: type an online player's name and pick 1 hour, 1 day or 1 week (renews an existing guest's pass); **Remove** ends one. Until it runs out (real time, so it counts while the server is off) the guest is a member: they send on it, hear it if encrypted, see a members-only note. Then they drop off, their blocks go silent or deaf, and both are told if online. Up to 8 per frequency; not the owner, friends or group members. Editing friends or handing over keeps them.
- **Intruder alerts (v0.36.0):**
  - When a non-member tries to use a private frequency, its owner gets a chat alert: a refused remote press, a sender they tried to tune onto it (tuner or converter screen), a block they placed on it that stays silent, or one they set to it that can't hear it (encrypted). No positions. At most once a minute per intruder, frequency and kind.
  - An owner who's offline gets them at login: the count and the last five, with in-game day and time (the last 16 are kept; older ones only counted).
- **Frequency log (v0.35.0):**
  - The audit's **Log** button (owner and operators) lists a private frequency's last turn-ons from every dimension, newest first: When (in-game "Day 1, 13:12"), What (Transmitter, Remote pulse, Remote toggle; "… on 2026" when a converter carried it over), Who (the transmitter's tuner or the remote's presser; "No record" for an old transmitter) and Where. Tooltips add how long ago it was. Clicking a row with a position lights a beam over it.
  - Like the audit, only the owner's, members' and no-record transmitters are located; another player's is named only, and where a remote was pressed is never stored. Each dimension keeps 32 per frequency (the pager catch-up's history); turn-ons saved before v0.35.0 show as "Turned on" with no cause.
- **Hand over a frequency (v0.34.0):**
  - The owner's audit has a **Hand over...** button: pick one of the frequency's friends or group members and click **Hand over** twice. They become its owner; everyone else on it, the old owner first, becomes their friend, so no block goes silent. Its name, note (members-only or not) and encryption stay. Chat messages to both.
  - Groups belong to their owner, so their members become single friends. Refused when the new owner is at the per-player limit, when it would have more than 16 friends, or for anyone but the owner (operators included).
- **Find it from the audit (v0.33.0):**
  - Clicking an audit row that shows a position puts a gold beam of light over that block for a minute, up to 8 at once; clicking it again turns it off. Rows with a beam get a gold edge, and the tooltip counts down ("Beam on for 58 more seconds"). A **Close** button next to Back closes every screen.
  - Only the clicking player's client draws them, and only over positions the audit already showed, so nothing new is revealed. They show past the fog, widen with distance, show in their own dimension only, and are cleared on leaving the world.
- **Members-only notes (v0.32.0):**
  - On a private frequency, the Note screen's **Who sees it** switch (Everyone / Members only) keeps the note to the frequency's members: the owner, friends, group members and operators. Nobody else's client receives it, so it's missing from their directory, Jade and tooltips; members see "(members only)" after it.
  - Membership changes give or take the note at once. Making the frequency public drops the switch for good, so a later claim doesn't hide it. On a public frequency the switch is greyed out.
- **Frequency audit (v0.31.0):**
  - Sneak-clicking one of your own private frequencies in the directory (an operator: any private one) opens a list of every block on it in every dimension: transmitters, repeaters and converters sending on it, converters listening on it, and loaded receivers (plain, toggle, compact), each with where it is, who tuned it and its status (On N / Off, Silent, Deaf). Updates every second.
  - The owner's and friends' blocks, and old ones with no recorded tuner (which still work on a private frequency), show their position (tooltip: distance and direction); other players' are only counted per player, kind and status ("Receiver ×2, Alex, Deaf"), so the audit can't locate anyone's base.
- **Frequency notes (v0.30.0):**
  - A short note per frequency (up to 64 characters, "lever by the front door; also lights the hall"), set with the **Note** button next to the tuner screen's Name box (a small screen: box, Save, Back; Enter saves; an empty box removes it). Notes belong to the world like names: shared, saved, valid in every dimension; on a private frequency only members and operators change them. At most 4,096 per world.
  - Shown in grey italics in the directory (after the name, in the tooltip, and matched by the search), in Jade under each frequency line, and in item tooltips (tuner, Remote Control, pager, radio blocks, converter, the Multi-Button Remote's selected button). The Note button is yellow, with the note as its tooltip, when the typed frequency has one.
- **Encrypted frequencies (v0.29.0):**

## How it works

- `SignalNetwork` is `SavedData`, one per dimension. It maps transmitter position → (frequency, power, reach), indexed by frequency, with a revision counter per frequency (not saved).
- **Repeaters and converters** are kept the same way: position → `Repeater(frequency, output, reach)` (a repeater has `output == frequency`), indexed both by the frequency they listen on and the one they send on, saved as `repeaters` (`output` only written for converters, so 0.20.0 saves load as repeaters). `RadioRepeaterBlockEntity` (shared by both blocks; a converter keeps its `output_frequency`) checks in on its first tick, when re-tuned and once a second; `onRemove` takes it out. What each sends isn't stored but worked out by `SignalNetwork.resolve(level)`:
  - each starts with what it hears straight from transmitters and remote signals on the frequency it listens on (`listenDirect`);
  - then, strongest first, each one that hears something passes that strength to the repeaters and converters inside its reach that listen on the frequency it sends on (a max-strength spread, O(n²) over the dimension). Because it only spreads out from real sources, a loop of repeaters, or of converters going A → B → A, with no source stays at 0.
  - One answer for the whole dimension, cached by `relayEpoch`, which goes up on any change to a relay and whenever a frequency some relay listens on is bumped; also re-worked after 20 ticks (for changes nothing reports, such as a remote's relay tower being broken). Stale entries (block gone, chunk loaded) are dropped while resolving.
  - `bump(frequency)` also bumps the output frequencies of converters listening on it, and so on down the line (with a visited set for loops), so receivers on a converted frequency re-scan as soon as the source changes.
  - `listen` = `listenDirect` plus every repeater/converter sending on that frequency whose reach covers the position (its resolved strength; counts toward `transmittersInReach`). So receivers, pagers and everything else built on `listen` hear them with no changes of their own.
  - `carried(level, frequency, coverage)` extends a coverage list through repeaters and converters (breadth-first over (frequency, area) pairs, each relay once, not re-adding one already relaying as a tower), giving the areas reached on each frequency plus how many repeaters and converters were added. `RemoteControlItem.sendSignal` uses it for "Heard by" (receivers and pagers on every frequency reached), the light and the "+ N repeaters + N converters" in the message; `recordTurnOn` stores a turn-on on every frequency it reached, so the pager catch-up covers converted frequencies too. `withRepeaters` is its same-frequency part, used by the locator.
  - `remoteCoverage` adds repeaters and converters as relay towers next to transmitters, and `RemoteSignal.reaches` accepts any of them (`SignalNetwork.isTower`).
  - The scanner's `bestTransmitters` also offers each repeater and converter covering the position, under the frequency it sends on (resolved strength, `Source.REPEATER` or `Source.CONVERTER`, added at the end of the enum since it's sent by ordinal). Ties go to the stronger, then nearer, then a transmitter. Tracking accepts any of them (`towerFrequencyAt` gives the frequency it sends on).
  - Converter screen: `RadioConverterBlock.useWithoutItem` sends `OpenConverterScreenPayload` (position, both frequencies, current strength, reach; block entities aren't synced) to the player; `ConverterScreen` sends `ConfigureConverterPayload` back, which only applies if `player.canInteractWithBlock(pos, 1.0)` and the block is a converter, then sets both with `setFrequencies`.
- **Transmitters** update their entry when:
  - a neighbor changes,
  - a mast in their column reports a change (masts walk up their column on neighbor change, placement and removal),
  - their frequency changes,
  - and once a second in their block entity tick, which also catches config changes and world edits.
- **Receivers** re-scan only when their frequency's revision changes or their own frequency changes, plus a once-a-second fallback. Changes arrive within one tick.
- **Toggle receiver (v0.23.0):** `RadioToggleReceiverBlock extends RadioReceiverBlock` and shares `RadioReceiverBlockEntity` (one block entity type for all three receivers). Each re-scan goes through `outputFor(heard)`: a plain receiver outputs what it heard; a toggle receiver remembers whether it heard anything last time (`lastHeard`, not saved) and flips `toggledOn` (saved as `toggled_on`, only when true) on silent → heard, outputting 15 or 0 through the same `POWER` state. `primed` (not saved) starts false on placement, load and re-tune (`onFrequencyChanged`), so the first check only records what it hears. Pulses bump the revision when they start and end, so even a 0.1s pulse is seen. Turn-ons while unloaded are missed. Jade and the readout use `isToggledOn()`.
- **Cleanup:** entries whose transmitter (or repeater/converter) block is gone are removed the next time something in range scans.
- **Dropped items:** the frequency rides on the item as the `reikos_signal_tower:frequency` data component (a converter also carries `output_frequency`). The block entity's `collectImplicitComponents` adds it, and the loot table's `copy_components` copies it onto the drop. `applyImplicitComponents` reads it back when the block is placed.
- **Tuner:** the screen sends `ConfigureRadioItemPayload` (hand, value, and the remote's mode and pulse length). The server checks the player is holding the right item and clamps the value. Tuning and copying happen in `FrequencyTunerItem.useOn` on the server. Radio blocks return `SKIP_DEFAULT_BLOCK_INTERACTION` for any held item so the tuner's `useOn` runs, and so masts can be placed against them.
- **Frequency names (v0.24.0):** `radio/FrequencyNames` is `SavedData` in the Overworld's data storage (`reikos_signal_tower_frequency_names`, a compound of frequency → name). `clean` drops characters chat can't show (`StringUtil.filterText`, so formatting codes go), trims and cuts to 24. The tuner screen sends `SetFrequencyNamePayload` on Done when the name box differs from the current name; the server applies it only if the player holds a tuner, then sends `FrequencyNamePayload` to every player. `PlayerLoggedInEvent` sends the whole list (`FrequencyNamesPayload`); `ClientPlayerNetworkEvent.LoggingOut` clears the client's copy. `FrequencyNames.nameOf(level, f)` reads the world's names for a `ServerLevel` and the client's copy otherwise (tooltips pass no level). `FrequencyNames.label(level, f)` is used as a message argument: the plain `Integer` when there's no name (so every message is unchanged), else a `frequency.reikos_signal_tower.named` component "%s (%s)" with the name in aqua. Server-built messages pass their level; `RadioFavorites.scroll` got a level overload for that. Jade's server data carries `name` / `output_name`, since the client copy could be missing. The scanner adds the name in front of a row's source; the remote and pager screens show it in place of the range hint; the converter screen next to its boxes.
- **Private frequencies (v0.25.0):**
  - `radio/FrequencyClaims` is `SavedData` in the Overworld's data storage (`reikos_signal_tower_frequency_claims`: a list of `{frequency, owner, owner_name, friends: [{uuid, name}]}`). `Claim.allows(uuid)` is true for the owner and friends. `FrequencyClaims.maySend(level, f, tunedBy)` is true when `tunedBy` is null (unknown tuner), the frequency is public, or the claim allows it. `refusal(level, f, player, key)` builds the red "private to X" messages.
  - `RadioBlockEntity` saves `tuned_by` (UUID, not copied onto the dropped item). The tuner (`FrequencyTunerItem.useOn`), the converter screen (`ConfigureConverterPayload`) and placing (`RadioBlock.setPlacedBy`) set it to the player; `onTunedByChanged` makes transmitters and repeaters check in again. The network's `Transmitter` and `Repeater` records carry `tunedBy` (saved as `tuned_by`; old saves load with none).
  - `SignalNetwork.blocked(level, f, tunedBy)` decides it per sender, on the frequency it *sends* on (a converter's output): `listenDirect` and `bestTransmitters` skip blocked transmitters; `resolve` gives blocked relays power 0 and never spreads to or from them; `listen` and `carried` skip them; `updateTransmitter` doesn't remember a blocked turn-on. Methods without a level use the network's own (`world`, set by `get(level)`; GameTests use `attachTo`). `isSilenced(level, pos)` is used by the readout, Jade (`private_to`, `silenced` in the server data) and the converter screen's packet.
  - Changing a claim calls `SetFrequencyPrivacyPayload.changed`: every dimension's network `invalidate(f)` (bumps `relayEpoch` and the frequency's revision, and so converters' outputs), so receivers, relays and lights re-check within a tick; then `FrequencyClaimPayload` (frequency + optional `ClaimInfo`) goes to every player. `PlayerLoggedInEvent` sends them all (`FrequencyClaimsPayload`); logging out clears the client's copy.
  - `SetFrequencyPrivacyPayload(frequency, private, friends)` from the tuner screen's Done (only when the button or box differs from the claim). Server: needs a tuner in hand; refuses anyone but the owner (operators may only make it public); for a new claim checks the per-player limit, the world limit and `otherSenders` (blocks in any dimension tuned by anyone else). Friend names are resolved among online players, or kept from the existing claim by name; unknown names and an over-full list are reported in chat.
  - Remote presses (`RemoteControlItem.sendSignal`, shared with the Multi-Button Remote) check the claim first and return the refusal without sending. `SetFrequencyNamePayload` refuses non-members unless they're operators.
  - The tuner screen adds a row under the Name box: the Private button and the Friends box, then a status line (public / yours / a friend's / someone else's / what Done will change). Someone else's frequency can't be edited (operators can still switch Private off), and its name box is read-only for non-members.
- **Wall Redstone, ceilings and corners (v0.52.0):** `FACING` is `DirectionProperty.create("facing", d != UP)` (old saves' horizontal values still load); `armDirection(facing, arm)`, `aroundCorner(level, pos, state, direction)` (edge not a conductor; the corner space's Wall Redstone with the same `attachedTo`, or `REDSTONE_WIRE` for UP), used by `connects`, `withConnections` and `calculateTargetPower`; `updateIndirectNeighbourShapes` redraws corner dust when this one changes (vanilla's own only looks for vanilla wire), and `neighborChanged` re-checks lines (for vanilla dust appearing over the top edge).
